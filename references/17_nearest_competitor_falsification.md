# 17 — Nearest-Competitor Falsification

Use before promoting any candidate to STRONG.

## Objective

Do not ask only whether the leading candidate fits.

Ask whether it fits materially better than the strongest plausible competitor.

## Competitor selection

Select the strongest alternative that:
- explains the same high-value clues;
- is not already rejected;
- is supported by current-image evidence or a search derived from current-image evidence;
- is not context-anchored.

Call this the `NEAREST_COMPETITOR`.

If no serious competitor exists after reasonable search, record `NO_SERIOUS_COMPETITOR_FOUND`.

## Differential test

Choose at least one check whose expected outcome differs between the leader and the nearest competitor.

A valid differential check must:
- be observable or externally verifiable;
- have high elimination power;
- not depend on the same evidence that generated both candidates;
- be specific enough that both candidates cannot easily pass for the same reason.

## Promotion rule

A leader may become STRONG only when one of these holds:

- nearest competitor is rejected by a differential check; or
- leader has a clearly stronger holistic match and the remaining competitor lacks a decisive expected feature that is confirmed visible under Expected Visibility; or
- no serious competitor is found after a targeted competitor search.

If the nearest competitor survives with similar explanatory power, do not return STRONG.

## Confidence-margin rule

Do not use numerical score margin alone as proof.

Score separation is supporting evidence only after differential falsification and holistic scene checks.
