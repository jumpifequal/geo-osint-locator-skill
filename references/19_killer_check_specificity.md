# 19 — Killer-Check Specificity

A killer check is not merely a strong-looking match.

It must discriminate the candidate from plausible alternatives.

## Valid killer check

A killer check must satisfy all:

1. candidate-specific;
2. falsifiable;
3. observable or externally verifiable;
4. high elimination power;
5. not redundant with an already-counted evidence channel;
6. expected to differ for the nearest competitor.

## Specificity classes

Assign:
- `UNIQUE`: expected to isolate the candidate from serious alternatives;
- `DISCRIMINATIVE`: expected to reject the nearest competitor;
- `GENERIC`: compatible with many candidates.

Only `UNIQUE` or `DISCRIMINATIVE` may satisfy the STRONG killer requirement.

`GENERIC` may support the score but cannot authorize STRONG.

## Failure mode

If the proposed killer check is merely:
- common to many sites;
- a broad category;
- a generic color/material;
- a non-unique skyline/landscape type;
- a feature already used to generate the candidate;

then downgrade it and choose another differential check.

## Competitor coupling

Before accepting a killer PASS, explicitly evaluate:

`Would the nearest competitor also plausibly pass this check?`

If yes, it is not a valid killer check.
